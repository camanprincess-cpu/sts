MID 18TH - 19TH CENTURY

1ST IR: STEAM & MECHANIZATION


Key Inventions:

James Watt's Steam Engine, Spinning Jenny, Power Loom, Steam Locomotive.

STS Impact:

Shifted human labor from rural farming to urban factory centers, initiating modern industrial capitalism.

Mechanized Steam Power
LATE 19TH - EARLY 20TH CENTURY

2ND IR: ELECTRICITY & MASS PRODUCTION

Standardized production and rapid global connectivity

Electrical Grids

Replaced clunky steam engines with continuous electric power in factories.

Assembly Lines

Henry Ford's moving assembly line drastically increased output speed.

Telecommunications

Telegraphs and telephones synchronized real-time trade worldwide.

MID-TO-LATE 20TH CENTURY

3RD IR: DIGITAL REVOLUTION


Core Driver:

Inventing silicon transistors, integrated microchips, and personal computers.

Societal Shift:

Transition from analog/mechanical hardware to digital information networks and the early World Wide Web.

Semiconductors & Digital Computing

Biotechnology

STS POSITIVE IMPACTS

SOCIETAL BUFFS OF INDUSTRIALIZATION

POSITIVE PARADIGM SHIFTS

  • Exponential Productivity: Massively scaled manufacturing capacity, making essential goods affordable.
  • Medical & Sanitation Growth: Chemical engineering brought antibiotics, modern vaccines, and water filtration.
  • Global Knowledge Network: Digital telecommunications connected research, education, and culture across borders.
  • Higher Living Standards: Elevated average global GDP per capita and reduced extreme manual toil.
STS NEGATIVE IMPACTS

SOCIETAL DEBUFFS & ETHICAL RISKS

CRITICAL CHALLENGES

  • Environmental Degradation: Anthropogenic global warming, deforestation, and industrial pollution.
  • Labor Exploitation & Displacement: Historical child labor in 1st IR and modern AI job displacement fears.
  • Digital & Economic Divide: Unequal global distribution of high-tech infrastructure and wealth gap.
  • Ethical & Privacy Risks: Algorithmic bias, mass data surveillance, and autonomous warfare threats.
THEORETICAL INSIGHTS

STS FRAMEWORK: TECH VS. SOCIETY

Does technology shape humans, or do humans shape technology?

CONCEPT A

Technological Determinism

Belief that technology drives social structure, cultural values, and human history autonomously.

CONCEPT B

Social Construction of Tech (SCOT)

Belief that human actions, politics, economic interests, and culture design and shape technology.

FINAL TAKEAWAY

CONCLUSION & SUMMARY

"Technology is a useful servant but a dangerous master."
As we navigate the 4th Industrial Revolution, balancing technological innovation with social ethics is key to a sustainable future.
